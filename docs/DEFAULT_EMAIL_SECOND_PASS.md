# Default email second review

Reviewed September 24, 2026. All 28 contact-level default emails across the 25 revenue-focus accounts were read again, including subjects, claims, positioning, proposed work and the final question. All 28 bodies received edits because the previous batch shared a repeated diagnostic CTA pattern. Existing subjects remain concise and relevant and were retained.

## Changes

The previous closing question used "still" in 25 of 28 messages, usually asking whether something remained manual. That made the recipient explain or admit a problem before seeing a reason to engage. None of the revised questions uses that pattern. Questions now offer a concrete workflow example or invite interest in help with a specific outcome. They do not ask for a meeting or require the recipient to design a project.

The required Nine-67 introduction remains explicit: AI-first company, forward-deployed engineers and custom software built with operating teams. It is shorter and its wording varies. Duplicated references to first versions, training and deployment were removed from the surrounding paragraphs. The message still explains that engineers take on delivery and work with users.

The proposed result is easier to picture: a checked request, repair packet, exception brief, sourced review, stock comparison or evidence review. Measurement is prospective. No savings or other results are guaranteed. Approval stays with the appropriate business or technical team.

Specific corrections include:

- Evans' TMS rollout is dated to 2025, without implying it is new.
- Gen Oil's ticket is checked before it becomes ready for billing.
- Braman's acquisition is not treated as proof that integration work remains open.
- Cayman scientific validation stays with scientists; evidence retrieval is the proposed help.
- Alta's onboarding findings remain attributed, and any cancellation effect would need testing.
- Michigan Box's missing specifications are flagged for review, not simultaneously described as complete.
- Chrysan's two contacts retain different operating and executive angles.

## Before and after examples

**Metro Wire, before:** "Does that checking still take much of your team's time?"

**After:** "Would a walkthrough of that request-to-quote process be worth sending?" The preceding paragraph now specifies the cable-family request check, approved product and stock records, and human review before pricing. The offer has a concrete output.

**Ansara, before:** "Is following up on those decisions still a manual job for your managers?"

**After:** "Would help turning those location reviews into completed actions be valuable to your managers?" The message describes the proposed review and names the actual work our engineers would take on.

**Braman, before:** "Is preserving that service context still relevant to your integration work?"

**After:** "Would an example of that customer-history brief be worth sending?" The body explicitly allows that the Fusion handoff may already be settled.

## Validation

Only contact subject/message fields and the matching top-level primary-contact aliases were in scope. All non-copy fields were compared before and after and remained identical. This includes company facts, sources, addresses, statuses, identities and writer-kit evidence.

All 28 bodies are unique and contain one final question, the required cold introduction and the reframe word overlap. No em/en dashes, fixed sender name, signature or unresolved sender placeholder was added. Bodies range from 89 to 105 words. All three tests in `src/lib/revenue-focus.test.ts` passed, covering copy lint, quality checks, sender composition, account consistency and address safeguards. A final independent editorial read prompted the Michigan Box clarification and helped distinguish offers to evaluate work from premature implementation asks.

## Limits

This review improves clarity, relevance and the reason to reply; it does not establish an optimal response rate. Existing account research does not prove a current pain, budget or intention to buy. A requested custom walkthrough or sample still needs to be prepared before a follow-up; the message does not say it already exists. Actual qualified and positive replies are needed to judge performance. LinkedIn messages and the four-version email bank were outside this file's edit scope.
