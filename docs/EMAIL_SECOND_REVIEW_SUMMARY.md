# Second email review, September 24, 2026

## Scope and purpose

This second review covers all 140 active email drafts: 112 saved variants and 28 default contact emails, across 25 companies and 28 contacts. The pass revised 65 saved variants and all 28 defaults, preserving 47 saved variants exactly. LinkedIn was reviewed separately in the preceding pass and is unchanged in this pass.

Three agents divided the work between saved variants, defaults and an independent recipient critique. The integrating editor challenged the final offers, subject lines and commitment requested in the closing question. The objective is a relevant positive reply that can lead to a business conversation, not merely agreement with an obvious statement or permission to receive more marketing.

## What the second pass found

- Several Personal emails repeated the proposed workflow in successive paragraphs, then asked the recipient to admit that work was still manual.
- Business idea messages often taught an experienced operator an obvious principle before asking permission to sketch it.
- Some replies would reveal little commercial interest: agreeing that a standard is sensible is not interest in getting help.
- Asking a stranger to choose an outside engineering team in the first reply is also a high hurdle. The versions need different ways into the conversation, not four versions of a sourcing decision.
- The better proof emails already gave a credible, accurately bounded reason to engage. Those should be preserved rather than rewritten merely to increase the change count.

## Editorial decisions

Personal: lead with relevant company or recipient context, offer one concrete improvement, and ask about that work without presuming an operational failure.

Business idea: state the proposed build and the business outcome directly. Explain how Nine-67 would deliver it with the operating team and how the first version would be assessed.

With proof: retain the actual example and distinguish the previous client engagement from a proposed use at the recipient. Do not promise equivalent timing or results.

Wildcard: keep a different but respectful framing. No jokes at the recipient's expense, manufactured urgency or assumptions that this is their first use of AI.

Default emails: keep the requested AI-first, forward-deployed engineering positioning while reducing repeated implementation/training prose. Preserve the researched reframe and clarify the output a leader would receive.

Send one selected version to a contact, record the actual version used, and distinguish relevant positive replies from refusals or automated responses.
