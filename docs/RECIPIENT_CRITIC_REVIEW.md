# Recipient review: cold email and LinkedIn

Reviewed 2026-09-24. Scope: all 28 named contacts at 25 companies, including four email variants, four LinkedIn variants, and one base/current email per contact: 252 draft placements. This document records the independent critique of the pre-revision copy. Channel editors received the findings while revising the drafts. It is an editorial assessment, not evidence of response performance. No reply-rate lift has been measured, and no wording can be certified optimal before sending and observing human replies.

## Highest-impact findings

1. Many messages ask a cold executive to choose a pilot, identify a representative record or describe their process before earning interest. That creates work for the recipient. First contact should make the relevant business idea understandable and ask one easy relevance or permission question. It should not ask for documents, a workshop or confidential examples.
2. LinkedIn frequently has two questions: an operational interrogation at the start followed by a generic CTA. Use one question, at the end. The repeated `A thought:` format and identical proof CTA across all 28 contacts make otherwise relevant work read like a mail merge.
3. Strong evidence is sometimes discarded. Alta's published onboarding finding, Cayman's scientific-validation reflection, Sprague's existing portal/logbook and Evans' existing TMS are materially more useful than inserting a company name into a generic AI pitch. Respect what they have already built.
4. Most proof variants lead with Nine-67 rather than the recipient. Start with the recipient's relevant operating situation, then give a brief, accurately scoped example. A professional-services application is analogous experience, not proof of results in pest control, manufacturing or distribution.
5. Some variants make the recipient design the value proposition: `What job would you put on that first assignment?` and similar open questions are harder to answer than a concrete choice about a clear workflow.
6. Some wildcard copy sounds dismissive of the recipient's existing competence. Avoid implying buyers forget available stock, tenants already manage their own maintenance, customers are argued with, or existing dashboards are useless. Interesting does not require an insult.
7. Base/current emails repeat long positioning and deployment paragraphs. Preserve the useful distinction: engineers learn the operation, build with users and stay through training. State it once, in ordinary language. Cold readers do need an introduction to Nine-67.
8. Braman email D contains a genuine coherence error: `That would be the first question...` has no preceding question. This is a required correction, not a stylistic preference.

## Evidence and claim boundaries

The review used the saved company trigger, source links, contact roles and reframe in `data/revenue-focus.json`, plus the authored draft banks and local case-study documentation. These are saved research inputs, not newly verified live facts. Public contact addresses remain published/unverified unless independently checked; copy review does not validate deliverability.

The user supplied the 20-application client claim and Nine-67's AI-first, forward-deployed engineering approach. Use the number on selected messages, not all. Resource allocation, collaboration, proposal, reporting, financial-planning and account-health examples must remain the actual Nine-67 examples. Do not convert a prior client's one-month deployment into a promise for this recipient. Do not add industry-specific outcomes, revenue gains, time savings or compliance claims without evidence.

Explicit limitations found: Chrysan's base email claimed a `relatively small team` without support in the saved trigger; Native's base email inferred growth from a leadership promotion; Bell's `working example` CTA could imply an already-built sector demo. Remove unsupported claims or describe a proposed illustration honestly. Evans' TMS implementation is from 2025; Braman's Fusion acquisition is August 2025. Neither is a newly discovered event in September 2026, and ongoing integration problems are unconfirmed.

LinkedIn drafts are direct messages, not short connection-request notes. Do not present them as fitting an invitation field or silently truncate them.

## Release and measurement criteria

- Every message introduces Nine-67 without assuming familiarity, gives one specific operating idea and ends with one answerable question.
- Variants differ in entry angle, not merely adjectives. Personal uses relevant observed context; Business idea explains one workflow; With proof uses matched, truthful experience; Wildcard offers a fresh but respectful framing.
- No em or en dashes, canned pleasantry after the CTA, fabricated urgency, claimed guaranteed results or unearned familiarity.
- No customer documents requested before interest. Offer only examples or outlines the team can actually provide.
- Preserve sender greeting, name, uploaded footer and version attribution. This review does not authorize changing recipient data or sent history.
- Compare human replies and positive replies by first-touch version, separating edited drafts and channels. Open detections are not proof of human attention. Small observational samples should not be described as randomized proof or a winning tone.

## Revised-copy challenge

After the channel rewrites, the independent critic reviewed all 112 LinkedIn messages again, all 112 email CTAs, all 28 email proof variants and the specifically flagged email bodies. The rewrite corrects the orphaned Braman reference, replaces document requests with easier replies, adds concrete workflows to previously vague LinkedIn messages and makes cross-industry proof explicitly analogous. Final challenge caught and returned grammatical errors introduced by email replacements; the malformed CTA patterns were subsequently corrected. It also removed unsupported sector-specific experience claims, Native growth inference, claimed tool availability and unnecessary personal-memory wording. Illustrative proposed outlines are distinguished from existing client case examples.

Remaining editorial tradeoff: many email Business idea and With proof versions use permission to send a sketch, so they are easier to answer but less varied in CTA structure. That is a testable choice, not a proven winner. The Personal and Wildcard versions supply distinct diagnostic and framing alternatives. The content is a stronger starting point for controlled reply measurement; it is not a certified maximum-response campaign.

### Final proof-CTA addendum

Independently re-read all 28 final With proof email bodies and their revised closing questions against `src/lib/outreach-proof.ts`. The final CTAs now offer the actual proposal, account-health or reporting example, or an outline of supported allocation, collaboration, finance or 20-application work. They do not claim that a prospect-specific application or demo already exists, do not imply the prospect has achieved the prior client's result, and do not introduce a new delivery timeline. Descriptive outlines can be prepared from the supplied delivery account; they should not be represented later as a published case study when none exists. This improves separation from Business idea's proposed-workflow sketch. No blocking unsupported claim or unavailable prospect-demo promise was found in this final 28-message pass.
